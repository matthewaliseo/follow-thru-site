How to Lock a Kid's iPhone Until Chores Are Done

Updated November 21, 2026

The rule is simple: no chores, no screen time. Keeping it is the hard part, because a rule only works if it's the same every day. Here are the three ways parents do it on an iPhone, and what each one is good at.

1. Apple's Screen Time, by hand

Every iPhone has Screen Time built in. With your child in your Apple Family Sharing group, you can set it up from your own iPhone in Settings, under Screen Time, by tapping your child's name. Screen Time Schedule (formerly Downtime) blocks apps during set hours. Time Allowances (formerly App Limits) cap apps or whole categories, like Games or Social, at a set amount per day.

To tie it to chores, you keep the limits tight and give more time by hand: your child asks for more time on their iPhone, and you approve the request once the chore is done.

Good at: free, built in, reliable. Harder: you're the one who checks every chore and every request, every day, and the amount you give is a guess each time.

2. A chore chart, and the rule out loud

A paper chart or a whiteboard by the fridge, plus the rule: screens after chores. It costs nothing and kids can see it.

Good at: simple, visible, no setup. Harder: nothing actually locks. The rule holds only as long as someone is home to hold it.

3. An app where kids earn the unlock

A newer kind of app uses Apple's Screen Time system to keep the apps you pick locked, and unlocks them when time is earned. Your child does the chore, sends it (with a photo if you ask for one), you approve it from your iPhone, and minutes go onto their balance. They spend minutes to open their apps, and when the minutes run out, the apps lock again on their own.

That's how CounterWait works. Kids can also earn with steps, workouts from Apple Health, or push-ups, squats and jumping jacks the iPhone camera counts. You set a daily earning limit, an optional daily spending limit and a bedtime, and you choose apps that never lock, like Messages or school apps.

Good at: the phone holds the line, not you. The rule is the same at 4 PM on a Tuesday as it is on Saturday. Harder: it takes a few minutes to set up on both iPhones, and it only limits iPhones, not iPads or computers.

Which one fits

  • If you're usually home and want zero setup, Apple's Screen Time with requests you approve works.
  • For younger kids who don't have their own iPhone yet, a chart is enough.
  • If you want the rule to run on its own, with you approving instead of policing, use an app where kids earn it.

Whichever you pick, research on parenting points the same way: clear rules, kept consistently, with warmth, work better than harsh or on-again, off-again ones (Pinquart, Developmental Psychology, 2017, 1,435 studies).

Questions

Can I lock my child's iPhone until chores are done without an app?

Partly. Apple's Screen Time can keep apps limited, and you can approve extra time by hand when a chore is done. Nothing ties the time to the chore itself, so you check and approve each one yourself.

Does CounterWait work with Family Sharing?

Yes. Family Sharing is recommended, never required. With your child in your Family Sharing group, only you can delete CounterWait or turn off its access on their iPhone.
