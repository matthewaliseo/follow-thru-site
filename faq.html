Basics

What is CounterWait?

CounterWait is an iPhone app that locks the apps you choose until you earn time for them. You earn minutes with exercise and tasks, then spend them to unlock your apps. When the time runs out, the apps lock again. It works for one adult on their own, for a family, or for two adults who keep each other accountable.

Who is CounterWait for?

Parents and guardians who want their children to earn screen time, adults who want to cut back their own screen time, and accountability buddies, like two friends, siblings or co-parents who check each other's tasks. It isn't a children's app: a parent or guardian, who must be 18 or older, sets it up and approves it on a child's iPhone. CounterWait's child setup is for ages 5 and older.

How is CounterWait different from Apple's Screen Time?

CounterWait works with Apple's Screen Time and uses it to lock apps. The difference is how time is given. Apple's Time Allowances (formerly App Limits) and Screen Time Schedule (formerly Downtime) set a fixed amount of time each day. In CounterWait, time is earned: walk, work out, do push ups, squats or jumping jacks in front of the camera, or finish a chore or task a parent, guardian or buddy approves. Earned minutes are spent to unlock apps for a session.

How do you earn minutes?

With steps, workouts, Exercise minutes and mindful minutes from Apple Health; with Push Ups, Squats, Jumping Jacks and Plank counted by the iPhone camera; and with chores and tasks. In a family, a parent or guardian approves a child's tasks and can ask for a photo of a finished task. A daily cap limits how much can be earned.

Does a treadmill workout count?

Yes, when an iPhone or Apple Watch records it. A workout started on the Watch, like Indoor Walk or Indoor Run, earns workout minutes, and the steps taken during it aren't paid again. Without a workout, the steps count like any others, as long as the iPhone is in a pocket or a Watch is worn. Holding the rails can stop the iPhone or Watch from counting steps, so starting a workout is the surest way.

Doesn't making screen time a reward make children want it more?

It can. Research on rewards (Deci, Koestner and Ryan, Psychological Bulletin, 1999) found that expected rewards can lower people's own interest in things they already enjoy, and putting screen time up as a prize may make it seem more valuable. CounterWait can't rule that out. What it does is put limits around it: a daily earning limit, an optional daily spending limit, a bedtime, and Apps That Never Lock for anything you don't want treated as a prize, like Messages or school apps such as Google Classroom or a calculator. If you want school hours covered too, you can keep Apple's Screen Time Schedule (formerly Downtime) on for those hours. Apps it blocks won't open then, even with earned minutes. You don't have to run everything through it. You can lock just a few apps, keep everyday chores as plain chores, and scale it back if you see it backfire.

Why doesn't CounterWait use streaks, leaderboards and surprise rewards to keep you hooked?

Because many apps make money from how long and how often you use them, so they're built to keep you coming back. A study of the apps young children used most found about 80% used design tricks to keep children playing or get them to pay: daily rewards for returning, characters that urge an idle child to keep going, fake countdown timers and pop-ups asking for purchases (Radesky et al., JAMA Network Open, 2022).

CounterWait's job is the opposite: less time on the phone. So it leaves those tricks out. There are no ads, no leaderboards and no saved rankings. The optional Face-Off game names a winner for one round, and nothing about it is kept. There's no streak to lose, either. The weekly goal starts fresh every Monday, since people who break a streak tend to do less of the activity afterward (Silverman and Barasch, Journal of Consumer Research, 2023). What you earn is plain: do the task, get the minutes, put the phone down.

What do I need to use CounterWait?

An iPhone with iOS 17 or later, signed in to iCloud with CounterWait turned on for iCloud. Family Sharing with the child in your family is recommended, so only you can delete CounterWait or turn off its access on their iPhone. Everyone who links up needs their own iPhone, and each child's iPhone needs to be signed in to the child's own Apple Account. CounterWait isn't on Android or iPad yet, and it only limits iPhones: a child's iPad or Mac isn't limited by CounterWait. iPad support is planned first.

Cost

How much does CounterWait cost?

Most of CounterWait is free, including earning, camera workouts, task approvals and parent/guardian rules. The free plan includes 2 people you check, 1 co-parent/guardian and 6 tasks of your own per person. CounterWait Plus raises the limits to 99 people you check and 99 tasks per person (still 1 co-parent/guardian), and adds the allowance tracker, the Home Screen and Lock Screen widget, and on an adult's own iPhone, the Flip Phone Mode widget. Plus is $2.99 a month or $19.99 a year, or $59.99 once for Lifetime, and it can be shared with Apple Family Sharing. The app is free during the public beta.

Privacy and Safety

Does CounterWait collect my data?

Very little, and none of it for ads. CounterWait runs no servers and uses no analytics, advertising or tracking tools. It never sells or shares your information. Your minutes, tasks and settings stay on your iPhone, with a backup in your own private iCloud. What you share with family or a buddy moves between your phones through Apple's iCloud, where we can't see it. One thing does reach us: when someone opens a buddy request link or scans an approval QR code, their iPhone leaves the invite, with an iCloud user ID Apple gives your account for this app (not your name, phone number or email), in the app's public iCloud database for up to 7 days. That's why the App Store privacy label lists User ID, used only so the app works, and not for tracking. Email you send us is the only other thing we get. The full details are in the Privacy Policy at counterwait.app/privacy.html.

Does the camera record my children?

No. When the camera counts reps, each frame is analyzed on the iPhone and then discarded. Workouts are never recorded, saved or sent anywhere, and the camera isn't used to recognize anyone.

Is exercise safe for everyone?

CounterWait isn't medical advice. Talk with a doctor before starting a new exercise routine, and stop if anything hurts. Children's camera workouts should be done with an adult nearby, in a clear space. If you've had an eating disorder, or exercise feels compulsive, set “Most from exercise” to 0 in Earning Rates and earn with tasks instead.

Can someone still get help when apps are locked?

Yes. In a crisis, call or text 988 (US) or your local emergency number. Phone and Emergency SOS never lock. Add Messages to Apps That Never Lock, so texting for help stays possible during grounding or Flip Phone Mode.

What does CounterWait do to protect children?

There are no ads and no tracking. A child's iPhone never shows purchases. A parent or guardian approves CounterWait's Screen Time access on the child's iPhone with their own Apple Account, and with the child in their Apple Family Sharing group, the child can't delete CounterWait or turn off its access without them. Children never see an adult's buddy activity. The app keeps a record of allowance owed but never holds or moves real money.

Is CounterWait cheat-proof?

No app is. CounterWait closes the easy shortcuts, like changing the phone's clock or typing workouts into Apple Health, and with Family Sharing a child can't delete it. What's left still takes real effort. If you notice a gap, email support@steppingstonegroup.net and we'll look into it.

Why can't my child delete apps?

While CounterWait is set up on a child's iPhone, no app can be deleted there, so a locked app can't be deleted and installed again to get around its lock. CounterWait also turns off Safari's Private Browsing, blocks adult websites, and keeps the date and time set automatically. These rules are explained in the app under How It Works. Reset App in CounterWait's Settings on the child's iPhone lifts all of them, and then CounterWait needs to be set up again on that phone.

Is CounterWait on social media?

No, on purpose. Social media is a big part of why CounterWait exists, so we don't use it. Any CounterWait page on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, X or anywhere else is fake. The only real places are counterwait.app, the App Store (Apple's TestFlight during the beta) and support@steppingstonegroup.net.

Troubleshooting

Why does the app list show apps I don't have?

The list of apps comes from Apple, not CounterWait. On a phone in a Family Sharing group, it can include apps from your family's iPhones, like your children's apps. Picking one that isn't on your iPhone changes nothing on it. To find your own apps, use the search bar at the top of the list. CounterWait never sees which apps you have.

Should I swipe CounterWait away to save battery?

No need. Apple says closing apps doesn't save battery, and CounterWait's locks keep working either way. But iPhone holds back updates from an app that's been swiped away until it's opened again, so approvals, new tasks and changes can arrive late on that phone. Open CounterWait and it catches up.

Why did a task or alert take a while to show up?

CounterWait has no servers: everything goes through iCloud, straight to the other iPhone. Most alerts come from iCloud itself and show within seconds, even with CounterWait closed. A few wait for iPhone to wake CounterWait, which it does on its own schedule, later when the app was swiped away, Background App Refresh is off, or Low Power Mode is on. Opening CounterWait on either phone checks right away. A task you add for a buddy says "Sent · Waiting for their phone" until it gets there, then "On their phone". The Guide explains it step by step.
