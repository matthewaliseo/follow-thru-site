Support

CounterWait locks the apps you choose until you earn time for them with exercise and tasks. Families and accountability buddies can approve each other's tasks.

CounterWait needs iCloud. Every iPhone that uses CounterWait must be signed in to iCloud, with CounterWait turned on for iCloud. Without it, locks, earned time, tasks and linked phones won't work. To check: open the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, then "See All" (or "Apps Using iCloud"), and make sure CounterWait is on.

Need help? Email support@counterwait.app. We usually reply within two business days. You can also tap Contact support in the app's Settings.

We're not on social media. On purpose. Social media is a big part of why CounterWait exists, so we don't use it. Any CounterWait page on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, X or anywhere else is fake. Don't give it your information, and please report it. The only real places are counterwait.app, the App Store, and support@counterwait.app.

Common questions

How do I set up a family? On your iPhone, choose "My Family: I'm a Parent/Guardian". In the Family tab, tap "Add Your Co-Parent/Guardian" or "Add a Child", then "Send Invite" and send it like a text. On the child's iPhone, with CounterWait installed and you there, choose "My Child: This Is Their Phone" and open the invite. Add the child to your Apple Family Sharing group if you can: then only you can delete CounterWait or turn off its access on their iPhone. The child's iPhone needs to be signed in to the child's own Apple Account, not yours. You can create one for them on your iPhone in Settings > Family.

Can my co-parent/guardian help? Yes. In the Family tab, tap "Add Your Co-Parent/Guardian". On their iPhone, they choose "Me, a Co-Parent/Guardian" and open the invite. You'll both get approvals. Invite lost? Tap "Add Your Co-Parent/Guardian" again to resend it.

How do I add an accountability buddy? On a parent/guardian's iPhone, open the Buddy tab and tap "Ask Someone to Check Me", "Check Someone Else" or "Check Each Other". On a "Just Me" iPhone, go to Settings > Accountability Buddies and tap "Ask Someone to Check Me" or "Check on Someone Else". Send the link in a message, or show the QR code if you're together. See How buddies work below.

How do I earn time? Walk or work out (counted from Apple Health), do Push Ups, Squats or Jumping Jacks in front of the camera, or finish tasks. If someone checks your tasks, they can ask for a photo of a finished task, or set a task to always ask for one. A daily cap limits how much you can earn.

How do I unlock apps? On Home, spend some of your minutes. The apps lock again when the time is up. Ending early refunds the minutes left.

What is the allowance tracker? With Plus, tasks can pay money as well as time. The app keeps a record of what's owed, and you pay outside the app in cash or with any payment app. CounterWait never moves real money.

Does it work on Android or iPad? Not yet. CounterWait locks apps through Apple's Screen Time and syncs through iCloud, so everyone in the family needs an iPhone for now. We know plenty of families mix iPhones and Android phones. We're a small, independent developer, and supporting Android well means building and maintaining a second app, which we can take on once there's enough support to sustain it. iPad support is planned first.

I forgot the parent/guardian passcode. On your iPhone, open the Family tab, tap your child's name, then Passcode and Privacy, and set a new one. Nothing on their phone is erased. We can't see or reset passcodes ourselves, because they're never sent to us. If that doesn't work, email support@counterwait.app.

How do I cancel Plus? Open the iPhone Settings app, tap your name, then Subscriptions, then CounterWait Plus.

How do I delete my data? In CounterWait, open Settings > Delete All My Data. To have a story sent with an earlier version or a share code removed, email support@counterwait.app.

Is CounterWait medical advice? No. Talk with a doctor before starting a new exercise routine, and stop if anything hurts. Parents and guardians decide what's right for their children and supervise their exercise. Children's camera workouts should be done with an adult nearby, in a clear space. If you've had an eating disorder, or exercise feels compulsive, set “Most from exercise” to 0 in Earning Rates and earn with tasks instead.

How buddies work

A buddy is another adult who helps you stick to your own screen time. Like a sister keeping you off social media, or two co-parents keeping each other honest. CounterWait isn't for keeping tabs on a partner. For a teen, a friend their own age is a fine buddy for cutting back together. If an adult checks a teen, it should be a parent, guardian or another adult in their family. An adult who asks a teen for personal photos, or to keep a secret, isn't safe. Children never see buddy activity.

Three ways to be buddies.

  • Someone checks you ("Ask Someone to Check Me"): your own tasks go to them for approval instead of counting right away.
  • You check someone ("Check Someone Else", or "Check on Someone Else" on a "Just Me" iPhone): you approve their tasks. Nothing changes for your own tasks: they still count right away when you tap them done, on the honor system.
  • You check each other ("Check Each Other", on a parent/guardian's iPhone): both of the above. Each of you approves the other's tasks, and neither can approve their own.

How many buddies? You can check more than one person, but only one person can check you at a time. To have someone else check you, stop sharing with your current buddy first. The free plan lets a "Just Me" iPhone check 2 people; Plus raises that to 99.

When someone checks you. Without a buddy, tapping one of your own tasks done counts it right away (after a few seconds to undo). Once someone checks you, tapping it opens "Send for Approval", which sends it to them. It shows as waiting in your task list, and the minutes come when they approve it. You can't approve your own tasks anymore. That's the point. Routines too, even small ones.

What a buddy who checks you can do. Approve your tasks or send them back with "Not Yet", ask for a photo of a finished task, and add tasks for you. They can also take away time, like for time past a limit you both set. They see your minutes earned and used this week, your reps, when you unlock, and when you change your settings or tasks. They can't change your limits, your locked apps or anything about your children.

If you're a parent/guardian. Nothing changes for your children. You still approve their tasks and set their rules, and your co-parent/guardian still can too. A buddy who checks you only sees your own tasks and screen time, never your children's.

Exercise. Exercise the camera counts (push-ups, squats, planks) and steps and workouts from Apple Health still earn right away, since they're already counted for you. The "Do 10 squats" task earns as the camera counts them.

Your unlocks. On a parent/guardian's iPhone, your buddy sees your unlocks only while "Limit My Own Apps" is on in Settings. Without it, your minutes have nothing to open.

Stopping. On a "Just Me" iPhone, and on a parent/guardian's iPhone with "Limit My Own Apps" on, tapping "Stop Sharing with" and your buddy's name waits 15 minutes before it takes effect, so it can't be done in a moment of temptation. While it waits, the ways to link a buddy are grayed out, and you can cancel. You never need your buddy's passcode to stop sharing, and they get one alert once you've left. If your buddy removes you instead, it ends without the wait, the next time CounterWait checks. After that, your own tasks count right away again. Tasks still waiting for their approval are dropped. Removing someone you check ends right away too, and changes nothing for your own tasks.

Setting up Apple's Screen Time

How does CounterWait use Apple's Screen Time? CounterWait locks apps through Apple's Screen Time. On a child's iPhone, a parent or guardian approves CounterWait's Screen Time access with their own Apple Account during setup. That approval is what lets CounterWait lock apps, and with the child in the parent or guardian's Family Sharing group, the child can't delete CounterWait or turn off its access without them. In the app, Settings > Apple's Screen Time > Show Me How walks through everything below.

What should stay on? Each child in your Family Sharing group, with Screen Time turned on for them (iPhone Settings > Screen Time > your child's name, or Settings > Family > your child's name > Screen Time). On your own iPhone, keep Screen Time on and CounterWait's access turned on.

Which of Apple's settings should I turn off? For the apps CounterWait manages, we recommend turning off Apple's own Time Allowances (formerly App Limits) and Screen Time Schedule (formerly Downtime). In iOS 27 they're in Settings > Family > your child's name. In earlier versions, look in Settings > Screen Time > your child's name. Apple's limits and CounterWait's locks both apply, and the stricter one wins, so if an Apple limit has run out, minutes earned in CounterWait can't open that app. This is the most common reason an unlocked app stays closed. It's a recommendation, not a requirement: keeping both works, as long as you expect either one to close the app. If you keep a Screen Time Schedule, add CounterWait to Always Allowed so it still opens to earn minutes. Adults using Just for Me can do the same for their own limits, in Settings > Screen Time.

What can stay on for safety? Apple's Content & Privacy Restrictions don't conflict with CounterWait, for example iTunes & App Store Purchases > Deleting Apps: Don't Allow, and age ratings. Ask to Buy and Communication Limits can stay on too.

Should Apple's Screen Time passcode match CounterWait's? No. Use a different one for each, so learning one doesn't give away the other. To protect Apple's passcode, also go to Settings > Screen Time > Content & Privacy Restrictions > Account Changes and choose Don't Allow. CounterWait's own passcode counts every wrong try, and after five in a row it stops taking tries for a while.

Can a child erase the iPhone to get rid of the locks? Not without the Apple Account password. With Find My turned on for the child's iPhone, erasing it asks for that password, and so does setting it up again afterwards. So keep the child's Apple Account password to yourself. If a child's iPhone stops checking in with CounterWait, your iPhone tells you after a day.

Can my child still call for help when everything is locked? Yes. In a crisis, call or text 988 (US) or your local emergency number. Phone and Emergency SOS never lock. Apple always keeps the Phone app open, so calls work no matter what CounterWait locks. Even without the passcode, tapping Emergency on the passcode screen lets them dial an emergency number. Emergency SOS works too: press and hold the side button and either volume button, and when the countdown ends, iPhone calls emergency services (see Apple's steps). Walk your child through it once: on the passcode screen, show them Emergency, without tapping Call. Add Messages to Apps That Never Lock, so texting for help stays possible during grounding or Flip Phone Mode. Add school apps they need too, like Google Classroom or a calculator, since grounding locks them otherwise.

Something feels wrong?

If someone uses CounterWait to pressure, threaten or scare you or your child, take screenshots first. Removing someone, or deleting data, can erase what they sent. We can't see what's shared in CounterWait, so we can't act on a report ourselves.

  • A child at risk: report it to NCMEC's CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org, or to your local police.
  • Domestic violence: contact The National Domestic Violence Hotline at thehotline.org.
  • In danger now: call or text 988 (US) in a crisis, or call your local emergency number.

Troubleshooting

  • Steps aren't counting: open iPhone Settings, then Health, Data Access & Devices, CounterWait, and turn on Steps and Workouts.
  • Apps aren't locking: open iPhone Settings, then Screen Time, and make sure Screen Time is on and CounterWait has access.
  • An app stays closed after unlocking: Apple's own Time Allowances (formerly App Limits) or Screen Time Schedule (formerly Downtime) may cover it. See Setting up Apple's Screen Time above.
  • The camera isn't counting reps: stand the phone up so your whole body is in view.
  • The invite link won't open: both phones need CounterWait installed and must be signed in to iCloud.
  • A child's phone says "Time to check for new rules": open CounterWait on that phone while it's on the internet. Wi-Fi alone doesn't clear it. If the lock screen's "Open CounterWait" button does nothing, Do Not Disturb or another Focus may be hiding its notification: open CounterWait from the Home Screen. See When an app is locked.
  • Approvals aren't arriving: open the app on both phones and pull down to refresh.
